MILLENNIUM ECOSYSTEM ASSESSMENT (2005) - SYNTHESIS ABSTRACT

ASSESSMENT OVERVIEW:
The Millennium Ecosystem Assessment was conducted between 2001 and 2005 to assess the consequences of ecosystem change for human well-being and to establish the scientific basis for actions needed to enhance the conservation and sustainable use of ecosystems. The MA responds to government requests for information received through four international conventions: the Convention on Biological Diversity, the United Nations Convention to Combat Desertification, the Ramsar Convention on Wetlands, and the Convention on Migratory Species. It is designed to meet needs of diverse stakeholders including the business community, health sector, nongovernmental organizations, and indigenous peoples.

SCOPE AND FOCUS:
The assessment examines linkages between ecosystems and human well-being, focusing on "ecosystem services." An ecosystem is a dynamic complex of plant, animal, and microorganism communities and the nonliving environment interacting as a functional unit. The MA deals with the full range of ecosystems—from those relatively undisturbed such as natural forests, to landscapes with mixed patterns of human use, to ecosystems intensively managed and modified by humans such as agricultural land and urban areas.

ECOSYSTEM SERVICES:
Four types of ecosystem services were assessed:
- Provisioning services: food, water, timber, and fiber
- Regulating services: climate regulation, flood regulation, disease regulation, waste treatment, and water quality maintenance
- Cultural services: recreational, aesthetic, and spiritual benefits
- Supporting services: soil formation, photosynthesis, and nutrient cycling

The human species, while buffered against environmental changes by culture and technology, is fundamentally dependent on the flow of ecosystem services.

HUMAN WELL-BEING FRAMEWORK:
Human well-being was defined as having multiple constituents:
- Basic material for a good life: secure and adequate livelihoods, sufficient food, shelter, clothing, and access to goods
- Health: feeling well, having healthy physical environment with clean air and water access
- Good social relations: social cohesion, mutual respect, ability to help others and provide for children
- Security: secure access to natural and other resources, personal safety, security from disasters
- Freedom of choice and action: opportunity to achieve what an individual values

The MA posits that people are integral parts of ecosystems and dynamic interactions exist between them and other ecosystem parts. The changing human condition drives, both directly and indirectly, changes in ecosystems and human well-being.

METHODOLOGY:
The MA synthesizes information from scientific literature and peer-reviewed datasets and models, incorporating knowledge from the private sector, practitioners, local communities, and indigenous peoples. Rather than generating new primary knowledge, it adds value to existing information by collating, evaluating, summarizing, interpreting, and communicating it in a useful form. The assessment applied expert judgment to provide scientifically credible answers to policy-relevant questions.

ASSESSMENT STRUCTURE:
The MA was conducted as a multiscale assessment with interlinked assessments at local, watershed, national, regional, and global scales. Four working groups prepared reports:
- Condition and Trends Working Group: assessed ecosystem state around year 2000
- Scenarios Working Group: explored plausible future changes during twenty-first century
- Responses Working Group: examined management options and opportunities
- Sub-global Assessments Working Group: captured lessons from regional assessments

CORE GUIDING QUESTIONS:
- What are current condition and trends of ecosystems, services, and human well-being?
- What are plausible future changes and consequent impacts on human well-being?
- What can be done to enhance well-being and conserve ecosystems?
- What are key uncertainties hindering effective decision-making?
- What tools and methodologies can strengthen capacity to assess ecosystems?

PARTICIPATION AND REVIEW:
Approximately 1,360 experts from 95 countries participated as assessment report authors, sub-global assessment participants, or Board of Review Editors members. The 80-member Board of Review Editors oversaw scientific review by governments and experts. All MA findings underwent two rounds of expert and governmental review, with approximately 850 individuals providing review comments.

CONCEPTUAL FRAMEWORK:
The MA framework depicts interactions between biodiversity, ecosystem services, human well-being, and drivers of change. Changes in indirect drivers (population, technology, lifestyle) lead to changes in direct drivers (fishing catch, fertilizer application), resulting in ecosystem changes that affect services and human well-being. These interactions occur across multiple scales and time periods, allowing for various intervention points to enhance well-being and conserve ecosystems.
