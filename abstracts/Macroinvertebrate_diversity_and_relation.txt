Macroinvertebrates are important components of biodiversity in headwater streams, and their diversity is driven by various environmental variables. The study was carried out in Toebirongchhu sub-watershed to study: (i) the macroinvertebrate diversity and composition within whole study area, three streams and the reaches, and (ii) relationship between macroinvertebrate diversity and environmental variables. The study area had low diversity considering Shannon-Weiner diversity (H'=1.69), but high while considering Simpson diversity (D'=.28), and a total of 829 individuals, 18 families and one class was recorded. Toebirongchhu stream had the highest H'(1.71) and Jichulum the lowest D'(.07) and the highest taxon richness (S=16), while reach T1 had the highest H'(1.72), lowest D'(.18), and the highest S (8) along with J1. Trichoptera (82.03%) the most and Plecoptera (0.24%) the least abundant order, while Hydropsychidae (37.0%) the most, and Athericidae and Polymitarcyidae (0.1%) the least abundant families. There was no significant variations in environmental variables among streams and reaches, and pH was slightly alkaline (7.67-7.97) and temperature ranged from 8-11.6°C, but conductivity was higher in Jichulum (J1=154.8 μS cm-1; J3=150.7 μS cm-1). Stream width had significant correlation with H' (τ=.511, p=.04), while conductivity had the highest correlation coefficient with S (τ=.69, p=.08) and high correlation with D' and J' (τ=-.48, p=.19). Among diversity indices S had stronger correlation with the maximum environmental variables showing it to be better diversity indicator in the study area.
