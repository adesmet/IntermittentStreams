This study examines the macroinvertebrate communities colonizing ephemeral winter-flooded pools in the River Frome floodplain at East Stoke, Dorset, UK. Observations were collected sporadically between 1999 and 2014 from 32 pools not exceeding 30 cm depth and 120 m2 in area. The pools varied in their connection to the river system, including isolated pools receiving only rainfall, connected pools that had received river water prior to sampling, and flowing pools receiving river water at the time of sampling. Temporal observations revealed rapid colonization by aquatic invertebrates including Chironomidae, caddisflies (Limnephilidae), and beetles (Helophorus spp., Hydroporus spp.), with community development truncated by habitat drying. Although individual pools supported relatively modest diversity (8-34 taxa), the collective pool mosaic supported 153 taxa overall, demonstrating the high biodiversity value of these ephemeral habitats. The study highlights that faunal composition is influenced by multiple interacting factors including proximity to the river, contribution of river water, time since flooding, season, climatic conditions, and year of sampling.
