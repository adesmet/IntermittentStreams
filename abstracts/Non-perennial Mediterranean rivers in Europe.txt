Non-perennial rivers and streams (NPRS) cover more than 50% of the global river network. They are particularly predominant in Mediterranean Europe as a result of dry climate conditions, further amplified by climate change and land use development. Historically, both scientists and policy makers underestimated the importance of NPRS for nature and humans alike, mainly because they have been considered as systems of low ecological and economic value. During the past decades, diminishing water resources have increased the spatial and temporal extent of artificial NPRS as well as their exposure to multiple stressors, which threaten their ecological integrity, biodiversity and related ecosystem services. In this paper, we provide a comprehensive overview about the structural and functional characteristics of NPRS in the European Mediterranean, and we discuss emerging tools for management. Because NPRS comprise highly unstable ecosystems, with strong and often unpredictable temporal and spatial variability – at least as far as we are able to estimate – we outline the future research needs required to better understand, manage and conserve them as highly valuable and sensitive systems. Efficient collaborative activities among multidisciplinary research groups aiming to create innovative knowledge are urgently needed in order to establish an appropriate methodological and legislative background. Close cooperation among scientists, EU and national policy makers and water management authorities are required in order to formulate the urgently needed economic alternatives to existing irrigated farming practices at the EU-Med scale, to reduce overall water consumption, in combination with more effective land use policies, and the linking of the current Common Agricultural Policy to the Horizon 2020 strategy. The incorporation of NPRS in forthcoming EU-Med River Basin Management Plans is a first step towards enhancing NPRS research and management efforts in order to effectively safeguard these highly valuable albeit threatened ecosystems.
