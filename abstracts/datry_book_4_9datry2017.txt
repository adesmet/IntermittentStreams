Intermittent rivers and ephemeral streams (IRES) are dynamic mosaics of lotic (flowing), lentic (nonflowing), and terrestrial habitats. Dynamic changes in hydrological connectivity shape population, community, and ecosystem patterns and underlying processes in IRES. Fragmentation can lead to thresholds and tipping points in metacommunity and metapopulation dynamics. The dynamic changes in connectivity make IRES ideal arenas for exploring temporal dynamics of metapopulations, metacommunities, and metaecosystems from novel perspectives. Conceptual developments in the dynamics of metapopulations, metacommunities, and metaecosystems have implications for IRES management and conservation (e.g., to define reference conditions and predict restoration trajectories).
