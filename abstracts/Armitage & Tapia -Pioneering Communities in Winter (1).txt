Pioneering communities in winter

The initial observations arose from simple curiosity: what animals colonised these new habitats during the winter period? Patrick Armitage and Gloria Tapia look at ephemeral ponds.

In the winter period, the fields in the Frame Valley adjacent to the East Stoke River Laboratory are subject to some degree of flooding. This can involve the whole floodplain but more often than not results in a series of small flooded pools. These pools may arise simply from heavy rainfall and are unconnected to the river, except in the most extreme floods, but others may receive water from the bankfull River Frame and adjacent Mill Stream.

A feature common to most of these habitats is ephemerality. The unconnected pools generally have a longer life than those receiving water from the main river and Mill Stream during high flow periods. The period of wetting is concentrated in the winter period although summer flooding can occur.

The initial observations arose from simple curiosity as to what animals colonised these new habitats during the winter period? How long did the habitats last and what changes occurred over time?

The literature on temporary waters and the adaptations of animals to them is extensive, and floodplain wetlands have been the subject of a range of studies including improvement of habitat for wading birds, the effects of irrigation on terrestrial vegetation and macroinvertebrates and more detailed work on colonisation and persistence of macroinvertebrate communities, but few have examined small ephemeral winter flooded pools. These are a particularly challenging area of study due to their relatively random occurrence, and short period of existence.

This article describes observations of the macroinvertebrate faunal communities taken sporadically between 1999 and 2014 in the River Frame floodplain adjacent to the River Laboratory at East Stoke Dorset.
