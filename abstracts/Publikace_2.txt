In Central Europe the effects of anomalous discharges on macroinvertebrate assemblages have been studied only in perspective of management of artificial discharges. However, recurrent drying-out of small streams in last decade showed the necessity to pay the attention to the impacts linked with global climatic changes. Therefore the objectives of this study were to describe the impact of drying-out on the composition of the mayfly taxocoene and to evaluate relevance of individual species traits for resisting of mayflies to drying-out. The mayfly taxocoene of two model localities, intermittent and permanent brooks, were investigated in 2005. Quantitative data from intensive sampling on intermittent stream showed a high fluctuation of total density depending on species life cycles and varying hydrological conditions. Comparing with the permanent stream, the mayfly taxocoene was deprived of rheobionts and high oxygen demand species, which meant considerable impoverishment of the taxocoene. To explain differences between both stream types in species survival and re-colonization ability, 25 species traits were evaluated. These included so-called "ecological traits" (e.g. habitat and substrate range, density, distribution, current speed adaptation, eurythermy) and "biological traits" connected with life cycle, adult stage, and larval stage. Only species showing the highest number of advantageous and minimum number of disadvantageous traits were able to successfully survive under unfavourable condition of intermittent brook. "Biological traits", which were considered to be the most important, could be good predictors for assessing the sensitivity of species to extreme changes of temperature and hydrological regimes and species ability to re-colonize streams exposed to these impacts.
