Different aquatic groups can regulate multiple ecosystem functions. However, the effects on ecosystem functions by interactions between different taxonomic groups operating on various functional levels are poorly known, especially in drying, intermittent rivers. Stream drying, as one of the most important stressors today, shapes communities inhabiting these ecosystems and substantially influences many community characteristics during and after drying, e.g. diversity, abundance, biomass, and productivity. Direct and indirect effects of drying-induced structural and functional reduction are subsequently remediated during the recolonization following flow resumption. This recolonization happens by various pathways (upstream or downstream migration, hyporheic or aerial recolonization), with potential implications for stream ecosystem functions through top-down or bottom-up interactions among key components differing in dispersal ability or dispersal pathways (e.g. macroinvertebrates and biofilms including algae, bacteria and fungi). As little is known about the importance of particular recolonization pathways, we designed an experiment focused on the description of the contribution of different recolonization pathways to post-drought remediation of stream functions. A great effort has been made to improve the experimental design, especially in construction and tuning the design of in-situ experimental devices that allow only one recolonization pathway at a time. The design and functionality of these devices were proven during the pilot experiment and subsequently adjusted based on our findings.
