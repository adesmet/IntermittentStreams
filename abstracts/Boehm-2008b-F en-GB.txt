Climate change: effects on Austrian water management

The latest research reports show what many people have been realising for years. Greenhouse gases released by humans are very likely the cause of the rise in average global temperatures observed since the middle of the 20th century. It is a generally recognised fact that global warming is influenced by humans and has become a challenge for the earth's inhabitants in the 21st century. The worldwide change in the climate system has an impact on nature, society, the economy and, in particular, on water management.

Although Austria is in a very favourable position compared to other regions in Europe with regard to the availability of water, it is important to monitor the possible changes caused by climate change and to take precautions for sustainable water management in current and future measures.

The description of the complex interrelationships of the climate shows how important it is to carry out long-term measurements and basic research in order to be able to precisely record and evaluate climate-related changes and their effects on water management.

The member states of the European Union have included the issue of climate change in the regulations that are important for water management - the Water Framework Directive and the Floods Directive - and have set themselves the task of incorporating adaptation strategies into their management and action plans.

The task of the ÖWAV working committee was to provide a factual presentation of the possible changes to the water management sectors and to differentiate between certain and uncertain effects. This aim is to provide an overview of the current state of research on the effects of climate change on the water industry in Austria and to create a neutral basis for further projects and measures.

This compilation of the changes in the water balance in Austria caused by climate change provides an orientation as to the direction in which this change can go.
