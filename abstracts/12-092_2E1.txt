Title: Delivering on a promise: integrating species traits to transform descriptive community ecology into a predictive science

Authors: W. C. E. P. Verberk, C. G. E. van Noordwijk, and A. G. Hildrew

Source: Freshwater Science, 32(2):531-547

DOI: http://dx.doi.org/10.1899/12-092.1

Abstract: A total of 71 species of caddisflies was collected during a 3-year-study by light traps and larval samples from 52 mostly unpolluted brooks in the Lower Rhine area (30-70 m a.s.l.). Almost half of the streams run dry in summer for several months because of the specific geological and hydrological conditions. Caddisflies of these temporary brooks show several adaptations associated with colonization of such habitats which are reflected, for example, in the flight periods: a short flight period in early spring followed by a larval quiescence in summer or an extended flight period from spring into autumn with diapausing adults are typical for 73% of species which occur primarily in temporary brooks. Permanent streams are colonized by species which have their flight periods in summer or in autumn without diapausing adults (96%). Autecological studies of the rare phryganeid species Oligostomis reticulata L. in temporary brooks showed a new type of life cycle with a long larval quiescence in the dry stream bed.
