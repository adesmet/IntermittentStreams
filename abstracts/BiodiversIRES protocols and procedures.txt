BiodiversIRES: Global biodiversity of intermittent rivers and ephemeral streams

Rivers and streams are among the most endangered hotspots of biodiversity on Earth. Intermittent rivers and ephemeral streams (IRES) are abundant on Earth, representing approximately half of our river network length. Historically, IRES have been considered as a subclass of rivers with low values for society, and this perception persists in many countries where governments and lobbies attempt to remove IRES from jurisdictional protections. This is mostly based on the assumption that IRES have low biodiversity values. However, recent evidence from local studies has shown that IRES can be rich in both aquatic and terrestrial organisms.

To better recognize the biodiversity of IRES and its underlying drivers, a global, standardized and coordinated effort is needed. This project aims to quantify aquatic and terrestrial biodiversity in IRES at the global scale by sampling over 200 IRES across the globe using standardized sampling techniques and protocols, including Surber sampling and dry sediment rewetting for aquatic invertebrates, pitfall traps for terrestrial invertebrates, soils and sediment eDNA metabarcoding for micro-organisms and meiofauna, water eDNA metabarcoding for fish, and phototraps and track analyses for mammals and megafauna. At each sampling reach, relevant environmental factors will be measured or estimated including flow regime, climate condition, geomorphic setting, and land-use. Global maps of IRES will also be generated under current conditions and future climate change scenarios to predict future riverine biodiversity of IRES.
